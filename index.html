About

From the QCD critical point to neutrino oscillations

I am an experimental physicist with a passion for understanding the quark–gluon plasma, heavy-ion collisions and neutrino physics.

My PhD work with the NA61/SHINE collaboration at the CERN SPS in Geneva focused on the search for the critical point of strongly interacting matter, using intermittency analysis of protons. I presented this work at many international conferences.

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Faculty of Physics, University of Warsaw. In the Polish academic system my position is adiunkt, a post comparable to assistant professor. I work on neutrino oscillations with the T2K, Super-Kamiokande and Hyper-Kamiokande collaborations in Japan, on the joint analysis of beam and atmospheric neutrinos.

Neutrino oscillations

Joint fits of T2K accelerator neutrinos and Super-Kamiokande atmospheric neutrinos to measure CP violation, the mass ordering and the mixing angles.

  • Momentum-scale systematics shared between beam and atmospheric samples
  • Cross-checks between independent fitting frameworks
  • Preparing for Hyper-Kamiokande, the next-generation detector
PhD · CERN SPSNA61/SHINE

The QCD critical point

Looking for power-law fluctuations of protons in nucleus–nucleus collisions, a predicted signature of a critical point in the phase diagram of strongly interacting matter.

  • Proton intermittency with scaled factorial moments
  • Monte Carlo simulation and data reconstruction
